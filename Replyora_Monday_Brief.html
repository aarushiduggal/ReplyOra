01 Founder Spotlight

Building Replyora in public

Five founder-story ideas for the week. People don't follow software — they follow the person building it. Document the journey, show the work, let the wins and the misses be the content.

Idea 01 · Origin

"I started Replyora because I watched good businesses lose customers in their DMs."

Talk to camera: the moment you realised a salon / clinic was losing booked revenue to a 6-hour reply. End on the mission — every business deserves a reply in seconds.

Format: 30s founder reel · Why: origin stories build trust faster than features · CTA: "Follow the build."

Idea 02 · Behind the build

"Watch me train an AI assistant on a real business in 10 minutes."

Screen-record building a demo bot (e.g. a physio clinic) — pasting their info, setting the tone, testing a question. Narrate the choices.

Format: sped-up screen reel · Why: shows the product + your expertise · CTA: "Want one for your business? Comment DEMO."

Idea 03 · The unglamorous truth

"What week one of cold outreach actually looks like."

Show the real spreadsheet of leads, the DMs sent, the silence — then the first reply. Honest, relatable, anti-highlight-reel.

Format: text-over-B-roll reel · Why: vulnerability + grind is highly shareable · CTA: "Save this if you're building something."

Idea 04 · The experiment

"I let my AI answer my DMs for 24 hours. Here's what happened."

Founder experiment with a verdict. Show real (anonymised) replies the assistant handled, what it nailed, what you'd tweak.

Format: talking-head + screen cutaways · Why: proof through self-experiment · CTA: "Book a demo to see yours."

Idea 05 · The lesson

"The first time a prospect said no — and what it taught me about the product."

Pick a real objection ("will it sound robotic?") and tell the story of how it changed how you build and demo. Turns a rejection into authority and pre-handles the objection for the next prospect.

Format: 45s story reel or LinkedIn post · Why: founders who share lessons read as credible, not salesy · CTA: "What objection should I tackle next? 👇"

06 LinkedIn

Seven posts, founder-grade

Professional but human. On LinkedIn you're building authority and attracting partners and higher-value clients — teach, tell the truth, and let the product be the proof.

Founder posts
Founder · 01

"Most small businesses don't have a lead problem. They have a response problem."

Open with that line. Share the stat that ~78% of customers buy from whoever replies first, then your take: the cheapest growth lever isn't more ads — it's answering faster. Close: "What's your average reply time, honestly?"

Founder · 02

"I built an AI that replies to customers in seconds. The hardest part wasn't the tech."

The hard part was trust — convincing owners it sounds human. Share how you solved it (training on their voice, human handoff). Close: "Where do you draw the line between automation and the human touch?"

Founder · 03

"A prospect told me no this week. It made the product better."

Tell the objection story and what you changed. Founders who share lessons (not just wins) earn credibility. Close: "Founders — what's a 'no' that improved your product?"

AI education posts
Education · 01

"'AI agent' vs 'chatbot' — the difference that actually matters for your business."

A chatbot answers; an agent acts — it qualifies, captures and books. Give a 3-line example. Position the shift as why 2026 is different. Close: "Is your tool answering, or actually doing the work?"

Education · 02

"The 24/7 reply is the fastest ROI in small-business AI. Here's why."

Enquiries arrive after hours; humans sleep. Walk through the math: X enquiries/week × Y% lost to slow replies × average sale. Close with the hybrid model — automate routine, escalate complex.

Startup journey posts
Journey · 01

"Week [n] of building Replyora in public."

Share one real number (DMs sent, demos booked), one win, one lesson. Keep it plain and specific. Close: "Building something too? What did this week teach you?"

Journey · 02

"How I'm finding my first 10 customers (the unsexy version)."

Outreach, free demos, niche focus, follow-ups. No growth-hack mythology — just the grind. Close: "Early-stage founders — DM me, happy to share the outreach approach."

09 Competitor Intelligence

What's working for the brands you watch

The signature high-performing format from ten relevant brands — what makes it work, the psychology underneath, what to borrow, and how to do it better. (These are the brands' proven content patterns; verify live engagement before citing specific numbers.)

ManyChat

"Comment [WORD] and I'll DM you…"

Why it works: turns a post into a list + a live demo of automation. Psychology: tiny action + curiosity + reciprocity.

Steal: your "Comment DEMO" mechanic — use it on every post. Improve: deliver a real mini-audit in the DM, not just a link.

Intercom

Opinionated "support is changing" POV posts

Why it works: a clear stance defines the category. Psychology: identity & agreement — people share what they believe.

Steal: take a stance — "speed beats everything." Improve: pair the opinion with a 10-sec proof clip.

HubSpot

Educational carousels & free templates

Why it works: pure value earns saves, shares and authority. Psychology: reciprocity + utility.

Steal: a weekly educational carousel (Section 08). Improve: make it niche-specific so it out-targets their generic.

Zapier

"Automate this workflow" demos

Why it works: concrete, repeatable, immediately useful. Psychology: "I could do that" aspiration.

Steal: show one flow — enquiry → qualified → booked. Improve: tie it to revenue, not just time saved.

Notion

Aesthetic templates + aspirational order

Why it works: beautiful + useful + identity. Psychology: aspiration + belonging.

Steal: lean into your premium look — it's your edge over "techy" rivals. Improve: add a clearer next step; they're soft on CTA.

Canva

"Did you know Canva can…" feature reels

Why it works: quick wow + instantly actionable. Psychology: novelty + instant payoff.

Steal: "did you know your AI can…" reels. Improve: frame as the customer outcome, not the feature.

Buffer

Transparent data & build-in-public

Why it works: openness builds trust. Psychology: authenticity.

Steal: share your real (small) numbers. Improve: connect each data point to an action the viewer can take.

Apollo.io

Tactical "cold outreach that works" content

Why it works: concrete B2B value. Psychology: utility for sales-minded viewers.

Steal: share your DM-outreach learnings (you're doing it daily). Improve: show receipts — a real reply beats generic advice.

Lindy AI

"AI employee does the task" agent demos

Why it works: rides the agentic wave with concrete demos. Psychology: future-shock + relief.

Steal: "AI that works while you sleep" positioning. Improve: ground it in a relatable small-business task, not enterprise.

Chatbase

"Build your own chatbot" demos

Why it works: shows ease and breadth. Psychology: empowerment.

Steal: simple "watch it get built" clips. Improve: emphasise done-for-you + human voice — your moat over their DIY.
